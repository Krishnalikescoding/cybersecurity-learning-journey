# Communicating with Stakeholders After an Incident

## 1. Case Study: Juliana Soto

After escalating two security incidents, Juliana's manager asks her to prepare reports for different stakeholders.

## 2. Communicating About Incident #1

**Incident:** An employee's account was locked due to multiple failed login attempts.

### Reporting Requirements
- The manager needs data showing how many employee accounts were locked in five departments over the last month.
- The report will be presented to senior executives of those departments.

### Communication Method: Visual Dashboard
Juliana creates a visual dashboard containing:
- Charts and graphs.
- Number of locked employee accounts.
- Department-wise comparisons.
- Data covering the previous month.

**Why a dashboard?**
- The information is primarily numerical.
- Charts make comparisons easier.
- Stakeholders can quickly understand the findings and decide what action to take.

## 3. Communicating About Incident #2

**Incident:** An attacker attempted to compromise a system storing customers' private data.

### Reporting Requirements
The CISO wants a detailed explanation of:
- What happened during the incident.
- Which security processes and procedures worked well.
- Which procedures need improvement.
- What the security team, data owners, and data processors could have done differently.

### Communication Method: Detailed Document

Juliana prepares a document containing:
- A timeline of events.
- Details of the attack attempt.
- Evaluation of existing security procedures.
- Recommendations for improving security.
- Responsibilities of the security team, data owners, and data processors.

She shares the report with her manager for review before it reaches the CISO.

**Why a detailed document?**
- The incident requires deeper analysis.
- The CISO needs information about security procedures and their effectiveness.
- A timeline helps explain the sequence of events clearly.

## 4. Comparison of Communication Methods

| Incident | Stakeholder Focus | Communication Method |
|---|---|---|
| Incident #1: Locked employee accounts | Numerical data and department-wise comparisons | Visual dashboard |
| Incident #2: Attempted compromise of customer data | Incident timeline, security procedures, and improvements | Detailed document |

## 5. Key Takeaways

- Communication should be tailored to the stakeholder's interests and responsibilities.
- Visual dashboards are effective for presenting numerical data and comparisons.
- Detailed documents are useful for explaining complex security incidents.
- Senior executives may focus on trends and business impact.
- CISOs may require detailed information about security controls, procedures, and improvements.

**Remember:**
- **Numbers and comparisons = Visual Dashboard.**
- **Detailed incident analysis = Written Report.**

The best communication method depends on what the stakeholder needs to understand and decide.
