# Effective Communication with Security Stakeholders

## 1. Get to the Point

Security stakeholders have time-sensitive responsibilities. Communication should be clear, brief, and actionable.

Before communicating, ask yourself:

1. **What?** – What information does the person need to know?
2. **Why?** – Why is this information important?
3. **When?** – When does the person need to take action?
4. **How?** – How can I explain the situation without technical jargon?

## 2. Follow Communication Protocols

When joining a security team, understand the organization's communication policies.

Learn:
- Which communication channels are approved.
- How security incidents should be reported.
- Which procedures must be followed when contacting stakeholders.

**Common communication channels:**
- In-person meetings.
- Video conferences.
- Emails.
- Company chat applications.

Always follow organizational communication protocols.

## 3. Communicate with Impact

Different stakeholders have different priorities.

| Stakeholder | Information They Focus On |
|---|---|
| Operations Manager | Day-to-day operations, system issues, and log anomalies |
| Security Risk Manager | Security threats, vulnerabilities, and potential risks |
| CISO | Overall security posture and cybersecurity risks |
| CFO | Financial impact of security incidents |

### How to Communicate Effectively

- With an **Operations Manager:** Explain relevant system issues, suspicious log activity, and operational impact.
- With a **Risk Manager:** Explain potential threats, vulnerabilities, and their impact.
- With a **CISO:** Focus on major security risks and the organization's overall security posture.
- With a **CFO:** Focus on financial losses and business consequences.

**Important:** Avoid overwhelming stakeholders with unnecessary technical details. Share information relevant to their responsibilities.

## 4. Communication Methods

Choose the communication method based on the type and complexity of information.

| Method | Best Used For |
|---|---|
| Instant Messaging | Quick updates and straightforward messages |
| Email | Detailed explanations and formal communication |
| Video Calling | Discussions requiring interaction |
| Phone Calls | Urgent or immediate communication |
| Spreadsheets | Sharing large amounts of data and numbers |
| Slideshow Presentations | Presenting findings, trends, and analysis |

### Choosing the Right Method

- **Simple message:** Instant message or phone call.
- **Complex situation:** Email or in-person meeting.
- **Large amounts of data:** Spreadsheet or graphs.
- **Detailed findings:** Slideshow presentation.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- Keep stakeholder communication clear, concise, and actionable.
- Understand the organization's communication protocols.
- Tailor information according to each stakeholder's responsibilities.
- Avoid unnecessary technical jargon.
- Select the communication channel based on urgency and complexity.

**Remember:** Communicate the right information, to the right person, at the right time, using the right method.
