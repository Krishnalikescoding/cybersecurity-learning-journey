# Security Stakeholders

## 1. Who Are Stakeholders?

A **stakeholder** is an individual or group that has an interest in an organization's decisions or activities.

**In cybersecurity:** Stakeholders receive security updates, make decisions, manage risks, and help protect the organization's assets.

## 2. Levels of Security Stakeholders

### 1. Cybersecurity Risk Manager
- Identifies, assesses, and mitigates security risks.
- Helps the organization reduce potential threats.

### 2. Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
- Highest-ranking person in an organization.
- Oversees the organization's overall direction and major decisions.

### 3. Chief Financial Officer (CFO)
- Oversees the organization's financial activities.
- Focuses on the financial impact of security incidents.

### 4. Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
- Highest-level security stakeholder.
- Oversees the organization's overall cybersecurity strategy, policies, and security posture.

### 5. Operations Manager
- Oversees day-to-day security operations.
- Leads teams responsible for implementing security strategies.
- Coordinates responses to security issues and incidents.

## 3. Stakeholder Communication for Entry-Level Analysts

Entry-level security analysts commonly communicate with:

- **Operations Managers:** Receive security reports and coordinate actions to resolve issues.
- **Risk Managers:** Receive information about potential threats and security risks.

When reporting an incident, analysts should clearly explain:
- What the security issue is.
- Its possible causes.
- Which systems or assets are affected.
- Its potential impact.

### Example: Multiple Failed Login Attempts

1. An analyst detects repeated failed login attempts on an employee's account.
2. The analyst reports the issue to the operations manager.
3. The operations manager contacts the employee's supervisor.
4. They determine whether the attempts were accidental or indicate account compromise.
5. They assess the impact of possible account lockouts on business operations.
6. The security team may implement preventive measures.

## 4. Communication Flow Between Stakeholders

Information generally moves through different organizational levels:

**Entry-Level Security Analyst**
↓
**Operations Manager / Risk Manager**
↓
**CISO / CFO**

- Analysts report security events to operational or risk management teams.
- Operations and risk managers coordinate responses and communicate broader security updates.
- CISOs and CFOs receive higher-level information about the organization's security posture and financial risks.

## 5. Key Differences

| Stakeholder | Main Focus |
|---|---|
| CEO | Overall organization and business decisions |
| CFO | Financial impact of security incidents |
| CISO | Overall cybersecurity strategy |
| Risk Manager | Identifying and reducing security risks |
| Operations Manager | Day-to-day security operations |

## 6. Key Takeaways

- Stakeholders are individuals or groups interested in an organization's decisions and activities.
- Entry-level analysts usually communicate with operations managers and risk managers.
- Analysts must clearly communicate security issues, possible causes, and potential impacts.
- Operations managers coordinate teams to resolve security issues.
- Senior stakeholders like CISOs and CFOs receive broader security updates through management.

**Remember:** Security analysts identify and report issues, managers coordinate responses, and senior stakeholders oversee the organization's overall security and business risks.
