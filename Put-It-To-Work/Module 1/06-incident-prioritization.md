# Security Decisions and Incident Prioritization

## 1. Your Decisions Matter

- Security analysts make daily decisions to protect an organization's systems and data.
- Entry-level analysts help identify and escalate potential security incidents.
- A major responsibility is recognizing which security events need immediate attention.
- Early escalation helps prevent minor security events from becoming major incidents.

## 2. Trust Your Instincts and Ask Questions

- Confidence is important when making security decisions.
- Learn the organization's **incident escalation policy** to understand how and when to report incidents.
- Ask questions whenever you are unsure about an incident or procedure.
- Asking questions demonstrates willingness to learn and helps prevent mistakes.

## 3. All Security Events Are Not Equal

Security analysts must understand which organizational assets and data are most important.

### How to Identify Critical Assets
1. Read the organization's onboarding materials.
2. Ask your supervisor about the most important assets and data.
3. Review the organization's security policies.

Understanding critical assets helps analysts prioritize incidents based on their potential impact.

### Common Incident Classification Types

**1. Malware Infections**
- Occur when malicious software enters an organization's computer or network.
- Can disrupt systems, steal data, or damage infrastructure.

**2. Unauthorized Access**
- Occurs when someone gains access to a system, application, or data without permission.
- Can result in data theft or system compromise.

**3. Improper Usage**
- Occurs when an employee violates the organization's acceptable use policies.
- Example: Using company resources for prohibited activities.

## 4. Incident Prioritization

Incidents should be prioritized according to their impact on critical business assets and operations.

**Example:**

| Incident | Priority |
|---|---|
| Unauthorized access to a critical manufacturing application | High |
| Malware infection on an unused legacy system | Lower |

Unauthorized access to the manufacturing application takes priority because it directly affects essential business operations.

**Important factors when prioritizing incidents:**
- Importance of the affected asset.
- Impact on business operations.
- Potential damage to data and systems.
- Severity and urgency of the threat.

## 5. Quick Escalation Tips

1. Familiarize yourself with the organization's escalation policy.
2. Follow the established procedures at all times.
3. Ask your supervisor questions whenever necessary.

## 6. Key Takeaways

- Incident escalation is an important responsibility of security analysts.
- Not every security event has the same level of risk.
- Critical business assets should receive higher priority.
- Understanding incident types helps analysts make informed decisions.
- When uncertain, consult a supervisor instead of guessing.

**Remember:** Prioritize incidents based on their potential impact on critical assets and business operations, not simply on how alarming they sound.
