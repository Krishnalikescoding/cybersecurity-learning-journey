# Data and Asset Classification

## 1. Data Classification

**Data classification** is the process of categorizing information based on its sensitivity and the level of protection it requires.

The four common data classification types are:

### 1. Public Data
- Information accessible to everyone.
- Poses minimal risk if viewed or shared publicly.
- Still requires basic security protection.
- **Examples:** Press releases, job descriptions, marketing materials.

### 2. Private Data
- Information that should not be accessible to the public.
- Unauthorized access can create serious risks for an organization.
- **Examples:** Company email addresses, employee ID numbers, research data.

### 3. Sensitive Data
- Information that must only be accessed by authorized individuals.
- Unauthorized access can cause significant financial and reputational damage.
- Includes:
  - **PII:** Personally Identifiable Information.
  - **SPII:** Sensitive Personally Identifiable Information.
  - **PHI:** Protected Health Information.
- **Examples:** Bank account numbers, usernames, passwords, Social Security numbers, passport numbers, medical records.

### 4. Confidential Data
- Important information essential to an organization's business operations.
- Access is usually restricted to a limited number of authorized people.
- May require signing an **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**, a legal contract requiring parties to protect confidential information.
- **Examples:** Trade secrets, financial records, sensitive government data.

## 2. Asset Classification

**Asset classification** means labeling an organization's assets according to their sensitivity and importance.

Assets are classified into different levels:

| Asset Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Low-level | Minimal impact if compromised or exposed | Public website address, marketing materials |
| High-level | Significant impact if exposed or compromised | Trade secrets, confidential financial records, sensitive internal emails |

### Low-Level Assets
- Usually contain publicly available information.
- Their exposure has minimal impact on the organization.
- Require basic security measures.

### High-Level Assets
- Contain sensitive or confidential information.
- Their exposure can cause:
  - Financial losses.
  - Damage to reputation.
  - Loss of customer trust.
  - Loss of competitive advantage.
- Require stronger security controls and restricted access.

## 3. Key Takeaways

- Organizations use data classification to determine how information should be protected.
- Public data has the lowest security requirements, while sensitive and confidential data require stronger protection.
- Asset classification helps identify which organizational assets are most important.
- Every organization has its own **data classification policy** defining how information is categorized.
- Security professionals must understand this policy to prioritize security measures and protect critical assets.

**Remember:** The higher the sensitivity and business importance of an asset, the stronger its security requirements should be.
