# Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Plans

## 1. Identify and Protect

Security teams follow a four-part process to protect an organization's assets:

1. **Identify assets:** Identify the important assets that need protection.
2. **Identify threats:** Determine potential threats that could harm those assets.
3. **Detect threats:** Implement tools and processes to detect potential threats.
4. **Create recovery plans:** Develop Business Continuity Plans (BCP) and Disaster Recovery Plans (DRP).

These plans work together to minimize the impact of security incidents on business operations.

## 2. Business Continuity Plan (BCP)

**Business Continuity Plan (BCP)** is a document that outlines procedures to maintain business operations during and after a major disruption.

**Purpose:**
- Minimize the impact of security incidents.
- Keep essential business functions running.
- Reduce financial losses and customer disruption.

### Four Essential Steps of BCP

1. **Conduct a Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**
   - Identify how disruptions to business functions could affect the organization.
   - Determine which operations are most critical.

2. **Identify, document, and implement recovery procedures**
   - Create actionable steps to recover critical business functions and processes.

3. **Organize a Business Continuity Team**
   - Bring together employees from different departments.
   - Typically includes Cybersecurity, IT, HR, Communications, and Operations.

4. **Conduct team training**
   - Train team members to handle different risk scenarios.
   - Practice responding to potential security incidents.

## 3. Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)

**Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)** outlines the steps an organization takes to recover its IT systems, software, hardware, applications, and data after a disaster or security incident.

**Purpose:**
- Minimize the impact of security incidents.
- Restore affected systems and services.
- Help resolve the security threat.

### Essential Steps of DRP

1. **Restore software:** Implement strategies to recover software and systems.
2. **Restore hardware:** Recover hardware functionality and infrastructure.
3. **Identify affected applications and data:** Determine which applications and data were impacted by the incident.

**Example:** A ransomware attack prevents a manufacturing company from accessing important production data. The DRP helps restore affected systems and data so operations can resume.

## 4. BCP vs DRP

| Business Continuity Plan (BCP) | Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) |
|---|---|
| Focuses on maintaining business operations. | Focuses on restoring IT systems and infrastructure. |
| Ensures critical business functions continue. | Recovers software, hardware, applications, and data. |
| Covers the wider organization. | Primarily focuses on technology recovery. |
| Works during and after disruptions. | Mainly focuses on recovery after an incident. |

## 5. Key Takeaways

- BCP and DRP are created together to reduce the impact of security incidents.
- BCP ensures that essential business operations continue.
- DRP focuses on restoring affected IT systems, applications, and data.
- Business Impact Analysis helps identify the effects of disruptions.
- Regular team training prepares employees to respond effectively.

**Remember:**
- **BCP = Keep the business running.**
- **DRP = Restore the systems and technology.**
