# Information Lifecycle Strategy

## 1. Case Study: Juliana Soto

Juliana Soto joins **Right-On-Time Payment Solutions** as a cybersecurity analyst.

The company provides:
- Money transfers between individuals.
- Payment processing services for businesses.

### Important Company Assets

Customers must provide the following information to use the service:

- Unique usernames and passwords.
- Full name or company name.
- Employee Identification Number (EIN) for business customers.
- Bank account information or debit card numbers.

**Key Observation:**
- The company handles large amounts of **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)** and financial data.
- This information is considered sensitive data.
- Unauthorized access can cause financial losses, harm customers, and damage the company's reputation.
- Therefore, customer data is the company's most important asset.

## 2. Information Lifecycle Strategy

The information lifecycle consists of four main steps:

### 1. Identify
- Identify the important assets of the organization.
- Focus on sensitive information that requires protection.
- **Examples:** PII, financial information, Social Security numbers, and EINs.

### 2. Assess
- Evaluate existing security measures.
- Review the organization's information security policies.
- Examine existing processes and procedures.
- Conduct vulnerability scans when appropriate.

### 3. Protect
- Implement security measures to protect identified assets.
- Monitor systems and networks.
- Learn existing security practices, preferably under the guidance of experienced analysts.

### 4. Monitor
- Continuously monitor implemented security controls and processes.
- Observe security systems for potential threats.
- Report findings to supervisors and identify areas requiring improvement.

## 3. Key Takeaways

- Identifying an organization's important assets is a major responsibility of a security analyst.
- Customer PII and financial information are critical assets for payment processing companies.
- Reviewing security policies helps analysts understand how organizational data should be protected.
- The information lifecycle provides a structured approach to protecting organizational assets.

**Remember the four stages:**

1. **Identify** – What needs protection?
2. **Assess** – What security measures are already in place?
3. **Protect** – How can we secure the assets?
4. **Monitor** – Are the security measures working effectively?
