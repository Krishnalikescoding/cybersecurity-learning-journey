# Attention to Detail and Incident Prioritization

## 1. Case Study: Juliana Soto

On her first day, Juliana:
- Reviewed company information to identify critical assets.
- Learned that the company handles customer PII and financial data.
- Developed an information lifecycle strategy.
- Started monitoring security systems and logs.

## 2. Security Events Identified

While monitoring logs, Juliana receives two security alerts.

### Event 1: Multiple Failed Login Attempts
- An employee's account was locked after **10 failed login attempts**.
- Company escalation policy requires this event to be reported to the **Password Protection Team**.
- It could indicate an attempt to gain unauthorized access.

### Event 2: Attempted Compromise of Customer Financial Data
- An unknown source attempted to compromise a system storing customers' bank account information.
- This system contains sensitive financial data and PII.
- A successful attack could affect hundreds of customers and seriously damage the organization.

## 3. Incident Prioritization

Juliana decides that Event 2 is more urgent because it directly threatens critical customer financial information.

| Security Event | Priority | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 10 failed login attempts | Lower | Requires escalation according to company policy, but affects an employee account |
| Attempted compromise of bank information system | Higher | Threatens sensitive customer data and critical organizational assets |

**Important:** Incidents involving critical assets and sensitive customer information should receive higher priority.

## 4. Escalation Process Followed

1. Identified both security events through system alerts.
2. Reviewed the company's escalation policy.
3. Prioritized the attempted compromise of the financial data system.
4. Notified the appropriate team members by following the established procedure.
5. Completed all required escalation steps for the customer PII incident.
6. Escalated the failed login event to the Password Protection Team.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- **Attention to detail** is essential for monitoring logs and identifying suspicious activity.
- Security analysts must understand the organization's escalation policy.
- Critical assets and sensitive customer information should receive higher priority.
- Analysts must follow the correct escalation procedures for each incident.
- Lower-priority incidents must also be investigated and escalated when required.

**Remember:** Identify → Analyze → Prioritize → Escalate → Follow the policy.

**Main lesson:** Handle the most critical threat first, but never ignore other security alerts.
