# Incident Escalation

## 1. Incident Escalation

**Incident escalation** is the process of identifying a potential security incident and transferring it to a more experienced team member or department for investigation and response.

**Example:**
- An employee repeatedly enters incorrect login credentials.
- A security analyst identifies the suspicious activity and reports it to the appropriate team.

**Purpose:** Ensure potential security incidents are investigated and handled by the right people.

## 2. Notification of Breaches

**Breach notification laws** require organizations and government entities to notify affected individuals when a security breach exposes their Personally Identifiable Information (PII).

**Examples of PII:**
- Social Security numbers.
- Driver's license numbers.
- Medical records.
- Home addresses.
- Other sensitive customer information.

**Security Analyst's Responsibility:**
- Understand the security laws applicable to the organization.
- Stay updated because these laws can change.
- Follow the organization's breach notification procedures.

## 3. Low-Level Security Issues

**Low-level security issues** are security risks that do not directly result in the exposure of PII.

Examples:
- One failed login attempt.
- An employee downloading unapproved software.
- Repeated incorrect password entries.

Although these issues may seem minor, they must be investigated because they can indicate larger threats.

### Examples of Potential Risks

| Security Issue | Possible Threat |
|---|---|
| Repeated failed login attempts | Brute-force attack or account compromise |
| Downloading unapproved software | Malware infection |
| Suspicious software on a work laptop | Compromise of the organization's network |

**Important:** A few failed login attempts might be accidental, but 15 failed attempts within 30 minutes could indicate a malicious login attempt.

Low-level issues should not be ignored. Early investigation helps prevent them from becoming major security incidents.

## 4. The Escalation Process

Every organization has its own **incident escalation policy**, which defines:

- Who should be notified when a security alert occurs.
- Who should be contacted if the first responder is unavailable.
- How incidents should be reported and escalated.

### Common Escalation Methods
1. IT help desk.
2. Incident management tools.
3. Direct communication with security team members.

Security analysts must understand and follow their organization's specific escalation procedures.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- Incident escalation ensures potential security threats reach the appropriate people.
- Breach notification laws require organizations to inform affected individuals when their PII is exposed.
- Low-level security issues must still be investigated.
- Repeated failed logins and unauthorized software downloads may indicate serious threats.
- Every organization has its own escalation policies and reporting procedures.

**Remember:** Never ignore a security alert just because it appears minor. Investigate it and escalate it according to organizational policy.
