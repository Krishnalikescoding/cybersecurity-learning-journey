# Security Controls and Information Privacy

## Overview

Organizations store information in many different places, creating pressure to implement effective security measures that protect information from theft or exposure.

**Security Controls:** Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks.

Security controls include a wide range of tools and practices that protect assets **before, during, and after** a security event.

Security controls can be organized into three types:

1. **Technical controls**
2. **Operational controls**
3. **Managerial controls**

## Types of Security Controls

### Technical Controls

**Technical controls** are technologies used to protect organizational assets.

Examples include:

- **Encryption**
- **Authentication systems**
- Other security technologies

### Operational Controls

**Operational controls** help maintain the day-to-day security environment.

These controls are generally performed by people.

Examples include:

- Security awareness training
- Incident response

### Managerial Controls

**Managerial controls** focus on how technical and operational controls are used to reduce risk.

Examples include:

- Policies
- Standards
- Procedures

An organization's security policy typically outlines the controls needed to achieve its security goals.

## Information Privacy

**Information privacy:** The protection of data from unauthorized access and distribution.

Information privacy is based on the **right to choose**. People and organizations should have the ability to decide:

- When private information is shared
- How it is shared
- To what extent it is shared

Security controls help regulate access to information and support information privacy.

## Example: Travel Application

Consider a travel application used to book a flight.

To reserve a seat, a customer may provide:

- Name
- Email address
- Credit card number

The airline needs access to this information to complete the reservation. However, not every employee should have access to all of it.

For example:

- A **marketing employee** does not need access to the customer's credit card information.
- A **customer support agent** may need access to relevant information while assisting with the reservation.
- The support agent should only have access to the information necessary for the specific task.

Security controls should therefore limit access according to the **user and situation**.

This is known as the **principle of least privilege**.

## Principle of Least Privilege

**Principle of Least Privilege:** The practice of limiting access so that users have only the access necessary for their role or situation.

Security controls should be designed with this principle in mind.

Applying least privilege requires organizations to distinguish between **data owners** and **data custodians**.

## Data Owners

**Data Owner:** A person who decides who can access, edit, use, or destroy their information.

Data ownership can become more complicated when information has multiple owners.

For example, an organization's **intellectual property** may have multiple data owners.

## Data Custodians

**Data Custodian:** Anyone or anything responsible for the safe handling, transport, and storage of information.

Data custodians can include:

- People
- Organizations
- Systems

Organizations and their systems can act as custodians of people's information in addition to individual people.

## Security Controls and Data Classification

Data is an **asset** and should be treated like other organizational assets.

Information privacy therefore requires appropriate:

- **Classification**
- **Handling**
- **Access controls**

Security controls should ensure that information is handled according to its classification and that access is limited appropriately.

## Key Takeaways

- **Security controls** are safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks.
- Security controls can be **technical, operational, or managerial**.
- **Technical controls** use technology such as encryption and authentication systems.
- **Operational controls** maintain day-to-day security activities such as awareness training and incident response.
- **Managerial controls** include policies, standards, and procedures that guide how risks are reduced.
- **Information privacy** protects data from unauthorized access and distribution.
- The **principle of least privilege** limits access based on the user's role and situation.
- **Data owners** determine who can access, edit, use, or destroy information.
- **Data custodians** are responsible for the safe handling, transport, and storage of information.
- Because data is an asset, it requires appropriate **classification and handling** to maintain privacy.
