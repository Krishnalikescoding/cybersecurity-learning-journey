# Information Privacy, Security, and Regulations

## Overview

Security and privacy are closely related concepts. People have the right to control how their personal data is collected and used, while organizations have a responsibility to protect the information they collect from being compromised or misused.

Understanding the difference between **information privacy** and **information security**, along with the regulations that govern data handling, is important for cybersecurity professionals.

## Information Security vs. Information Privacy

Although the terms are sometimes used interchangeably outside cybersecurity, they describe different functions.

**Information Privacy:** The protection of data from unauthorized access and distribution.

**Information Security (InfoSec):** The practice of keeping data in all states away from unauthorized users.

### Key Difference

- **Privacy** focuses on giving people control over their personal information and how it is shared.
- **Security** focuses on protecting people's privacy choices and keeping their information safe from threats.

### Example: Customer Data Collection

A retail company may want to collect customer information such as:

- Age
- Gender
- Location

Before collecting this information, the company should:

1. Disclose how the information will be used.
2. Obtain the customer's consent.
3. Provide an option for customers to opt out of sharing their data.

After obtaining consent, the company should implement security controls to protect the information from:

- Unauthorized access
- Unauthorized use
- Unauthorized disclosure

Security controls should also respect the privacy choices of customers and other stakeholders who choose to opt out.

> **Note:** Privacy and security are both essential for maintaining customer trust and brand reputation.

## Why Privacy Matters in Security

Data privacy and protection became increasingly important during the late 1990s as technology companies began moving beyond simply processing data to storing and using it for business purposes.

For example, companies began storing information about users' online searches and sharing access to this information with other companies to provide personalized experiences.

This created broader concerns about:

- Whether organizations should be allowed to collect private data.
- Whether organizations should be allowed to share private data.
- How securely collected information was being stored.
- The possibility of data being abused, misused, or stolen.

As organizations collected more data, they became more focused on transparency and security. Businesses began explaining how information was collected, stored, and used while implementing additional security measures.

Without clear rules, however, these protections were applied inconsistently.

> **Note:** The more data that is collected, stored, and used, the more vulnerable it can become to breaches and threats.

## Privacy Regulations

**Regulations:** Rules established by a government or another authority to control how something is done.

Privacy regulations help protect users from having their information collected, used, or shared without consent. Regulations may also specify security measures required to protect private information from threats.

Three influential regulations and standards are:

1. **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**
2. **Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)**
3. **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)**

## General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

**General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR):** A set of rules developed by the **European Union (EU)** that gives data owners significant control over their personal information.

Personal information covered by GDPR can include:

- Name
- Address
- Phone number
- Financial information
- Medical information

The GDPR applies to businesses that handle the data of **EU citizens or residents**, regardless of where the business operates.

For example, a U.S.-based company that handles the data of EU visitors to its website may be subject to GDPR requirements.

## Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)

**Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS):** A set of security standards developed by major organizations in the financial industry.

PCI DSS is designed to protect **credit and debit card transactions** from:

- Data theft
- Fraud

## Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)

**Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA):** A U.S. law that requires the protection of sensitive patient health information.

HIPAA prohibits the disclosure of a person's medical information without their knowledge and consent.

> **Note:** Although GDPR, PCI DSS, and HIPAA were developed by specific regions or industries, they can influence data-handling practices for organizations around the world.

## Regulatory Compliance

Organizations must comply with the regulations relevant to their:

- Industry
- Geographic area
- Applicable authorities

Other security and privacy laws also exist, so the specific requirements an organization must follow depend on its circumstances.

Regulatory compliance is important because it helps organizations protect sensitive data and demonstrate that they meet required security standards.

## Security Audits and Assessments

Organizations commonly use **security audits** and **security assessments** to evaluate their security controls and identify gaps.

### Security Audit

**Security Audit:** A review of an organization's security controls, policies, and procedures against a defined set of expectations.

Audits can be used to determine whether an organization meets specific compliance requirements.

### Security Assessment

**Security Assessment:** A check used to determine how resilient current security implementations are against threats.

Assessments help organizations identify weaknesses and improve their overall security posture.

## Audit vs. Assessment

| Evaluation | Purpose | Typical Frequency | Common Performer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Security Audit** | Reviews security controls, policies, and procedures against defined expectations. | Approximately once per year | Internal teams or external third-party groups |
| **Security Assessment** | Evaluates how resilient security implementations are against threats. | Approximately every 3–6 months | Typically internal employees |

### Example: Multi-Factor Authentication

Suppose a regulation requires **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** for all administrator accounts.

An organization could:

1. Conduct a **security audit** to check whether administrator accounts comply with the MFA requirement.
2. Conduct a **security assessment** to identify additional security weaknesses.
3. Discover that many users are using weak passwords.
4. Decide to enable MFA for all user accounts to improve the organization's overall security posture.

> **Note:** Compliance with legal regulations, such as GDPR, can be evaluated during security audits.

## Security Audits and Assessments in Practice

Security professionals may participate in both audits and assessments.

### Security Audits

Security audits are generally performed less frequently, approximately **once per year**.

They may be conducted:

- Internally
- Externally
- By third-party organizations

### Security Assessments

Security assessments are generally performed more frequently, approximately every **three to six months**.

They are typically conducted by internal employees and may be used to prepare an organization for a security audit.

Both audits and assessments help organizations determine whether their systems are effectively protecting information and privacy.

## Key Takeaways

- **Privacy** focuses on people's control over their personal information and how it is shared.
- **Information security** focuses on protecting data from unauthorized users and threats.
- Organizations should be transparent about how personal information is collected and used.
- Customers should be given appropriate choices regarding the sharing of their personal information.
- Major regulations and standards include **GDPR, PCI DSS, and HIPAA**.
- The specific regulations an organization must follow depend on its industry and applicable area of authority.
- A **security audit** reviews security controls, policies, and procedures against defined expectations.
- A **security assessment** evaluates the resilience of security implementations against threats.
- Audits and assessments help organizations identify security gaps and maintain effective privacy protections.
- Failing to address security weaknesses can contribute to serious consequences such as **fines and data breaches**.
