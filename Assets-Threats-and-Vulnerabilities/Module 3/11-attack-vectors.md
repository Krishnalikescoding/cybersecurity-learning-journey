# Threat Actors & Attack Vectors

## Attack Surfaces (recap)
- All potential vulnerabilities a threat actor could exploit (networks, servers, devices, staff).
- Defense strategy: **limit access** to these surfaces.

## Threat Actors
- **Threat actor**: any person/group posing a security risk (inside or outside org, intentional or accidental).

### 5 Categories (by motivation)
- **Competitors** – rival companies benefiting from leaked info
- **State actors** – government intelligence agencies
- **Criminal syndicates** – organized groups profiting from crime
- **Insider threats** – anyone with (or who had) authorized access; accidental or intentional
- **Shadow IT** – people using ungoverned tech (e.g., personal email for work)

## Hackers
- **Hacker**: anyone using computers to gain unauthorized access to systems/networks/data (umbrella term, doesn't capture intent alone).

### 3 Types (by intent)
- **Unauthorized (unethical/malicious) hackers** – commit crimes; skill varies
  - **Script kiddies** – low-skill, use pre-written malicious code from others
- **Authorized (ethical) hackers** – improve org security; internal teams, external vendors, freelancers
  - **Bug bounty programs** – companies pay hackers to find/report vulnerabilities
- **Semi-authorized hackers** – violate ethics but not malicious
  - **Hacktivist** – uses skills for political goals; exposes risks before malicious hackers do

## Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)
- **APT**: threat actor maintains unauthorized access to a system for an extended period.
- Mostly associated with nation-states/state-sponsored actors.
- Goal: surveil target, gather intel → manipulate government, defense, financial, telecom services.
- Private businesses aren't safe — often targeted first as a stepping stone to larger entities (since hitting government/utilities directly is costly/time-consuming).

## Access Points (Attack Vectors)
- **Direct access** – physical access to a system
- **Removable media** – USB drives, portable hardware
- **Social media** – communication/content platforms
- **Email** – personal and business accounts
- **Wireless networks** – on premises
- **Cloud services** – third-party provided
- **Supply chains** – third-party vendors as backdoors

- Matching a threat actor's intent to likely attack vector helps predict targets (e.g., remote workers → more likely email threat than direct access).

## Key Takeaways
- Defending starts with thinking like a threat actor.
- Not every threat actor intends harm.
- Matching intentions with attack vectors = key skill for an attacker mindset.
