# Security Risk, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

## Overview

Security professionals need to communicate and coordinate effectively when responding to security events. Understanding the relationship between **risk**, **threats**, and **vulnerabilities** provides a foundation for security planning and incident response.

## Foundational Security Terms

**Risk:** Anything that can impact the **confidentiality, integrity, or availability (CIA)** of an asset.

**Threat:** Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact assets.

**Vulnerability:** A weakness that can be exploited by a threat.

These terms have specific meanings in cybersecurity and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Security Risk

Security plans focus heavily on how an organization defines and manages risk. The definition and treatment of risk can vary between organizations because each organization values different assets and has different business requirements.

One way to calculate risk is:

**Likelihood × Impact = Risk**

- **Likelihood:** The probability that a negative event will occur.
- **Impact:** The potential effect of the event on an asset or organization.

### Example

When driving to work, there is a risk of being late.

- **Threat:** A nail puncturing a tire.
- **Vulnerability:** The tire being susceptible to sharp objects.
- **Impact:** Potentially being late enough to lose a job.
- **Likelihood:** Influenced by factors such as road conditions.

Businesses similarly evaluate security risks by considering the likelihood and potential impact of negative events.

### Why Organizations Calculate Risk

Risk calculations help organizations:

- Prevent costly and disruptive events.
- Identify improvements to systems and processes.
- Determine which risks can be tolerated.
- Prioritize critical assets that require attention.

The impact of a negative event depends on the asset involved and the situation.

As a security professional, an important focus is reducing the **likelihood** of security problems by addressing factors that increase the chances of an incident.

## Risk Factors

Two broad factors that contribute significantly to security risk are:

1. **Threats**
2. **Vulnerabilities**

The risk of an asset being harmed depends greatly on whether a threat can exploit a vulnerability.

### Relationship Between Threats and Vulnerabilities

A threat can take advantage of a vulnerability, increasing the likelihood that an asset will be harmed.

For example:

> A nail is a **threat** because it can puncture a tire. The tire's susceptibility to sharp objects represents a **vulnerability**.

In security planning, reducing vulnerabilities or limiting opportunities for threats can reduce the likelihood of the associated risk.

## Categories of Threats

**Threats** are circumstances or events that can negatively impact assets.

Threats are commonly categorized into two types:

| Threat Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intentional** | A deliberate action intended to negatively impact an asset. | A malicious hacker targeting a misconfigured application to access sensitive information. |
| **Unintentional** | An event or action that negatively impacts security without malicious intent. | An employee holding a door open for an unknown person, allowing access to a restricted area. |

Both intentional and unintentional threats can result in security events that require a response.

## Categories of Vulnerabilities

**Vulnerabilities** are weaknesses that can be exploited by threats.

They can generally be grouped into two categories:

| Vulnerability Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | A weakness in technology, such as software or system configuration. | Misconfigured software that allows unauthorized access to important data. |
| **Human** | A weakness resulting from human actions or behavior. | An employee forgetting or losing their access card. |

Both technical and human vulnerabilities can contribute to security risk.

## Relationship Between Risk, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

The three concepts are closely connected:

1. **Risk** represents the potential for an asset to be negatively impacted.
2. **Threats** are circumstances or events that can cause harm.
3. **Vulnerabilities** are weaknesses that threats can exploit.
4. When a **threat exploits a vulnerability**, the likelihood of harm to an asset increases.

Understanding these relationships helps security professionals identify, assess, and manage security risks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Risk** is anything that can impact the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an asset.
- **Threats** are circumstances or events that can negatively impact assets.
- **Vulnerabilities** are weaknesses that can be exploited by threats.
- Risk can be represented as **Likelihood × Impact = Risk**.
- Threats can be **intentional** or **unintentional**.
- Vulnerabilities can be **technical** or **human**.
- Understanding the relationship between risks, threats, and vulnerabilities is fundamental to effective security planning and analysis.
