# Asset Management and Asset Classification

## Overview

**Asset management** is the process of tracking assets and the risks that affect them.

> You can only protect what you know you have.

Identifying, tracking, and classifying assets are important parts of asset management. **Asset classification** helps organizations understand the sensitivity and importance of their assets so they can protect them appropriately.

## Why Asset Management Matters

Organizations need a workable system for managing and protecting assets while ensuring that business operations continue smoothly.

Effective asset management requires detailed knowledge of the assets within an environment.

For example, a bank needs to have money available each day to serve customers. The organization must have the necessary equipment, devices, and processes in place to ensure that money remains available and protected from unauthorized access.

## Types of Assets

Organizations may need to protect many different types of assets:

- **Digital assets:** Customer data, financial records, and other digital information.
- **Information systems:** Networks, software, and other systems that process data.
- **Physical assets:** Facilities, equipment, and supplies.
- **Intangible assets:** Brand reputation, intellectual property, and other non-physical assets.

Regardless of their type, assets should be classified and accounted for.

## Asset Classification

**Asset classification** is the practice of labeling assets based on their **sensitivity** and **importance** to an organization.

To classify an asset effectively, an organization generally needs to know:

- **What** the asset is
- **Where** the asset is located
- **Who** owns the asset
- **How important** the asset is

These characteristics help an organization determine the asset's sensitivity and value.

### Benefits of Asset Classification

Asset classification helps organizations:

- Implement effective risk management strategies.
- Prioritize security resources.
- Reduce IT costs.
- Maintain compliance with legal regulations.

## Common Asset Classifications

A commonly used classification scheme consists of four levels:

| Classification | Description |
|---|---|
| **Restricted** | The highest level. Used for extremely sensitive assets, such as need-to-know information. |
| **Confidential** | Assets whose disclosure could cause a significant negative impact on an organization. |
| **Internal-only** | Assets that are available to employees and business partners. |
| **Public** | The lowest level. Assets that can be released without causing negative consequences to the organization. |

The way these classifications are applied depends on the characteristics of the asset and the organization's requirements.

> **Note:** Classification schemes can vary between organizations. For example, government organizations may use **confidential** rather than **restricted** for their most sensitive assets.

## Challenges of Classifying Information

Identifying the owner of an asset can be straightforward for physical assets, such as a building. However, ownership can be more difficult to determine for information and devices.

### Example: Laptop Ownership

A business may issue a laptop to an employee for remote work. The business may be considered the owner of the laptop, but the employee might also use it for personal purposes, such as storing personal photos.

This creates questions about ownership and the appropriate classification of information stored on the device.

### Multiple Classification Values

Information can also contain elements with different sensitivity levels at the same time.

For example, a letter addressed to someone may contain:

- **Public information:** The recipient's name.
- **Confidential information:** The recipient's address, which may only be appropriate for certain people to access.

This makes information classification more complex than simply assigning one classification based on the entire asset.

## Key Takeaways

- **Asset management** involves tracking assets and the risks that affect them.
- Organizations must know what assets they have in order to protect them effectively.
- Assets can be **digital, information systems, physical, or intangible**.
- **Asset classification** labels assets according to their sensitivity and importance.
- A common classification scheme consists of **restricted, confidential, internal-only, and public**.
- Classification helps organizations prioritize security resources, manage risk, reduce IT costs, and maintain regulatory compliance.
- Identifying ownership and assigning classifications can be challenging, especially for information.
- A single asset or collection of information may contain data with different sensitivity levels.
