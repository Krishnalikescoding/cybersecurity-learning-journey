# Data and the Three States of Data

## Overview

Information is one of the most valuable assets organizations have. Most information exists in digital form, which is called **data**.

**Data:** Information that is translated, processed, or stored by a computer.

Because billions of connected devices exchange data continuously, protecting digital assets presents additional challenges compared to physical assets.

The protection of data depends on **where the data is and what it is doing**. Security teams commonly consider data in three states:

1. **Data in use**
2. **Data in transit**
3. **Data at rest**

## Three States of Data

### Data in Use

**Data in use** is data that is currently being accessed by one or more users.

**Example:**  
When you log into your email account and view your inbox, the information in your inbox is considered **data in use**.

### Data in Transit

**Data in transit** is data that is traveling from one point to another.

**Example:**  
When you send an email or message, the information being transmitted from your device to its destination is **data in transit**.

### Data at Rest

**Data at rest** is data that is not currently being accessed.

It is typically stored on a physical device or other storage system.

**Example:**  
After finishing with your email and closing your laptop, the data stored on the laptop is considered **data at rest**.

> **Important:** The state of data depends on what the data is doing, not simply on whether a physical device is being used or moved.

## Information Security

**Information Security (InfoSec):** The practice of keeping data in all states away from unauthorized users.

Weak information security can result in:

- Identity theft
- Financial loss
- Reputational damage

These consequences can harm:

- Organizations
- Business partners
- Customers

## Data and Cloud Storage

The concept of **data at rest** can become more complicated as technology changes.

Physical devices such as smartphones increasingly store data in the cloud. Therefore, information is not necessarily considered at rest simply because the physical device containing or accessing it is stationary.

Security professionals should remain aware of new vulnerabilities as systems become increasingly connected.

## Data States and Asset Management

Understanding the three states of data helps security teams:

- Analyze security risks.
- Determine appropriate asset management plans.
- Consider how data should be protected in different situations.
- Track information as part of an organization's overall security strategy.

The key principle is:

> **Protecting data depends on where the data is and what it is doing.**

## Key Takeaways

- **Data** is information that is translated, processed, or stored by a computer.
- Data exists in three commonly considered states:
  - **In use:** Being accessed by users.
  - **In transit:** Traveling from one point to another.
  - **At rest:** Not currently being accessed and typically stored.
- **InfoSec** focuses on protecting data in all three states from unauthorized users.
- Poor information security can result in identity theft, financial loss, and reputational damage.
- Cloud storage makes determining the state of data more complex.
- Understanding the three states of data helps security teams analyze risk and develop appropriate asset management plans.
