# Phishing

**Phishing:** The use of digital communications to trick people into revealing sensitive data or deploying malicious software.

Phishing is one of the most common types of **social engineering**. It exploits human error to gain:

- Private information
- Access to systems or accounts
- Money or other valuables

Phishing attacks often appear to come from a **trusted person or business**, causing people to act against their better judgment and potentially break security procedures.

# Origins of Phishing

Phishing has existed since the **early days of the internet** and can be traced back to the **1990s**.

As more people began using the internet, malicious actors recognized that the internet provided a degree of **anonymity** that could help them commit crimes.

## Early Phishing Attacks

One of the earliest examples of phishing targeted **AOL Instant Messenger (AIM)** users.

Users received emails asking them to:

- Verify their accounts
- Provide personal billing information

The messages were actually sent by malicious actors pretending to be AOL service providers.

### Mass Phishing

**Mass Phishing:** Attacks that send malicious emails to a large number of people in the hope that some recipients will fall for the attack.

During the AIM attacks, attackers carefully designed emails to look like legitimate AOL messages.

They used:

- Official-looking **logos**
- Similar **colors**
- Similar **fonts**
- Requests for account information

Attackers used stolen information to create **fraudulent AOL accounts**, which could then be used to commit other crimes anonymously.

In response, AOL changed its security policies and began displaying warnings to users about phishing attacks.

# How Phishing Has Evolved

Phishing continued to evolve as businesses and technologies became more digital.

During the **early 2000s**, e-commerce and online payment systems became increasingly popular. Online transactions created new opportunities for attackers to steal information and commit crimes.

Five major types of phishing that security analysts should know are:

1. **Email Phishing**
2. **Smishing**
3. **Vishing**
4. **Spear Phishing**
5. **Whaling**

## 1. Email Phishing

**Email Phishing:** A phishing attack carried out through email in which threat actors pretend to be a trusted person or organization.

Email phishing remains one of the **most common forms of phishing**.

Originally, attackers often used email phishing to steal:

- Account credentials
- Credit card information
- Other sensitive information

Later, email phishing also became a popular method for distributing **malicious software** and infecting computer systems and networks.

### Example

An attacker may send an email pretending to be from a bank and ask the recipient to click a link and verify their account credentials.

# 2. Smishing

**Smishing:** A type of phishing that uses **Short Message Service (SMS)** and other text-based messaging services.

Smishing can include attacks delivered through:

- SMS text messages
- Apple's iMessage
- WhatsApp
- Other messaging platforms

### Example

A victim may receive a text message pretending to be from a bank or delivery company that asks them to click a link and provide personal information.

# 3. Vishing

**Vishing:** A phishing attack that uses **voice calls or voice messages** to trick targets into providing personal information over the phone.

### Example

An attacker may pretend to be a bank employee and call a customer asking for account information or verification details.

# 4. Spear Phishing

**Spear Phishing:** A form of email phishing in which **specific individuals or groups are deliberately targeted**.

Unlike mass phishing, spear phishing is more **customized and targeted**.

### Example

An attacker may specifically target the **accountant of a small business** with an email designed to appear as though it came from the business owner.

# 5. Whaling

**Whaling:** A type of **spear phishing** that targets **high-ranking executives** within an organization.

Whaling attacks focus on individuals who may have significant authority or access to valuable information.

### Example

An attacker may send a carefully crafted email to a company's CEO pretending to be another senior executive.

# Phishing in the Early 2000s

By **late 2003**, attackers were creating fraudulent websites that looked similar to legitimate businesses such as **eBay** and **PayPal**.

Attackers also launched mass phishing campaigns against:

- E-commerce websites
- Banking websites
- Online payment systems

These campaigns were increasingly used to distribute **malicious programs**.

# Recent Phishing Trends

Beginning in the **2010s**, attackers increasingly moved away from mass phishing campaigns and began using more **targeted phishing attacks**.

**Targeted Phishing:** Attacks sent to specific targets using highly customized methods to create a strong sense of familiarity.

Attackers use information about their targets to make phishing attempts appear more legitimate.

## Angler Phishing

**Angler Phishing:** A targeted phishing technique in which attackers impersonate **customer service representatives on social media**.

This technique takes advantage of people's tendency to publicly complain about businesses online.

### How Angler Phishing Works

1. A customer posts a complaint about a business on a message board, comment section, or social media platform.
2. A threat actor finds the complaint.
3. The attacker contacts the customer while pretending to be a customer service representative.
4. The attacker uses a fraudulent account that looks similar to the real business account.
5. The attacker offers to fix the customer's problem.
6. The victim is tricked into providing **sensitive information**.

Angler phishing is similar to the early AIM attacks because attackers use **fraudulent accounts that appear legitimate** to gain the victim's trust.

# Types of Phishing at a Glance

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Email Phishing** | Uses email to impersonate a trusted person or organization |
| **Smishing** | Uses SMS and other text messaging services |
| **Vishing** | Uses voice calls or voice messages |
| **Spear Phishing** | Targets specific individuals or groups with customized attacks |
| **Whaling** | Targets high-ranking executives |
| **Angler Phishing** | Impersonates customer service representatives on social media |

# Key Takeaways

- **Phishing** is a common form of social engineering that tricks people into revealing information or installing malicious software.
- Phishing has existed since the **1990s** and continues to evolve.
- **Mass phishing** targets large numbers of people.
- Modern phishing increasingly uses **targeted and customized attacks**.
- The major types include:
  - **Email phishing**
  - **Smishing**
  - **Vishing**
  - **Spear phishing**
  - **Whaling**
  - **Angler phishing**
- There is **no perfect technological solution** that can completely prevent phishing.
- **Security awareness and training** can reduce the impact of phishing attacks.
- Security professionals may be responsible for creating training programs that help employees identify phishing and other forms of social engineering.
- Sharing knowledge helps create a stronger **culture of security**.
